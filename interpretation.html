Our Interpretation

Janhvi’s not trying to solve beauty, she’s trying to make it livable.

At the heart of this is a paradox she refuses to simplify.

She can love glamour and still be serious, she can be soft, but still be in control. She can play with the male gaze, but still belong to herself.

This push and pull, this tension, is the spine of the brand.

The product is not the end goal; it’s the entry ticket to a safe space that’s warm, honest, and unabashedly feminine. Non-performative, non-judgemental, inclusive. It’s the feeling of being around women you trust, where you can ask the ‘stupid’ question, share the trade secret, and not be judged for wanting to look and feel good.

It’s belonging, not just aesthetics. It’s sisterhood. A space where beauty feels like it belongs to women first, away from performance, away from permission.

The moodboards communicate this. They’re not selling seduction, they’re selling ‘aliveness’, feminine Indian sensuality that feels confident, unabashed and comfortable in your own skin. There’s also a protected quality to the world depicted, away from the male gaze, this feels like a tropical vacation; sun, water, fruit, warmth, natural, ease.

Beauty as a small escape from real life worries.

The emotional world here keeps landing as three things: warmth, ritual and light.

  • Warmth as aliveness, sun on skin, ease.
  • Ritual as shared inheritance, woman to woman, tips and truths passed along without judgement.
  • Light as the physical language of this intimate world, where everything blooms.

Tension as a motif, returns here as well. The best of both worlds, international quality with Indian beauty secrets; global taste, but rooted in nani dadi nuske. Soft and premium, alive not sterile, indie beauty carving out its very own space.

Packaging, as a signal, communicates this as well. The energy in the references shared read, soft and curvy, feminine with undertones of imperfection and sexy. Not loud or aggressive and definitely not trying too hard.

It should feel like it belongs in a woman’s world, not like it’s asking for permission to exist.

Three Guiding Principles

If we had to distill what’s in the manifesto and moodboards into 3 guiding principles, it would be this:

  • 01

    Embrace the paradox

    Glamour and seriousness, softness and control, play and self-possession. We don’t try to resolve this, instead, we build a brand that can carry this.

  • 02

    Build sisterhood as the real destination

    The product is just the entry ticket, the end goal is belonging. Warmth, honesty, no gatekeeping, no judgement. Just beauty that feels like it belongs to women first.

  • 03

    Make transparency a signature behaviour

    Anti-mythmaking, clear human truth, no fear-mongering, no preaching, a wink, not a lecture.

A Founding Principle

Transparency as a Founding Principle

And for this world to feel real, it needs a throughline that is practical and grounded, not just poetic.

Because a safe space isn’t built with vibes, it’s built with trust. The lived kind. The kind you earn by telling the truth, by naming trade-offs, by not pretending that everything is magic, and by never making women feel foolish for asking basic questions or for wanting more.

This is what Janhvi calls out in her manifesto. She’s fiercely anti-mythmaking, she wants to tell us what’s real, what isn’t, what works, what doesn’t, what’s risky and what’s not worth it. As this is something we believe in as well, we have to translate this ethos into a founding principle, a brand signature.

Which means it has to show up in the product and branding decisions we make, and in how we explain these decisions to our community.

It means we talk about key ingredients, including the ones people love to hate, without fear or defensiveness. If we choose to use something like silicone, we don’t hide it or apologise for it, we explain what it does, why it’s there, who it benefits, who may want to avoid it, and what the trade-off would be if we removed it.

It means we explain the technique and formulation logic behind our products in human language. What texture and finish we were aiming for. What constraints we had, what we tried and rejected and why. What we prioritised and what we chose not to.

It means we make trade-offs visible. Why this ingredient percentage, and not higher or lower. Why this shade selection and not others. Why this packaging format and not something else. Where our time, effort, iteration and quality focus actually went.

Transparency should also reduce anxiety, not create it or amplify it. Where there are multiple options, we help people choose with their best interests in mind. We explore minis so people can try without committing upfront to something they’re not sure about. We build simple selection aids that feel like a friend is helping, not an algorithm that’s pushing you deeper into the funnel.

This signature carries into our imagery, packaging and comms as well.

We need to align on what we will show and say and what we never will. What the product is actually like to use in real life. How it sits on skin in normal lighting because everything looks incredible in controlled lighting and a perfect setup. What’s edited, what’s not. What we will never fake, exaggerate or hide. Why we love and use the products we make, and are proud to recommend you add them to your collection.

And because this is a community we want to protect, transparency has to be a two-way contract. We should expect honest questions and we should offer honest answers. When our product isn’t right for them. When someone is pissed off or confrontational.

We should also protect our community from bullying disguised as honesty, moral policing, unverified fake content and performative outrage, even if it means losing some people along the way.

We also need to define our boundary line. What we won’t share and why. Not because we want to hide something or gatekeep, but because our promise has to be sustainable. We protect people’s privacy, what’s shared in confidence, and we protect innovation and IP.

Transparency is what protects this world from becoming performative. It’s what keeps sisterhood from being an empty promise, and turns it into behaviour. It’s through our implementation of transparency that we make our brand paradox livable, instead of just surface level brand speak. It forces us to be clear, consistent and human about what’s real, what isn’t, what works, what doesn’t, what you need and what you don’t, at all.
